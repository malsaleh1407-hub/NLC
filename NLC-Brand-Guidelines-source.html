Brand Essence
Who we are
Engineered light for a country in motion.
National Lighting Company is a Saudi-owned LED manufacturer and Schneider Electric authorized panel assembler. Founded in 1993 in Dammam, NLC delivers lighting and integrated power systems to commercial, industrial, healthcare, and government projects across the Kingdom.
Every brand decision in this guide ladders up to three ideas: national pride, engineering precision, and human warmth. Navy carries the first two. Orange carries the third.
National
Saudi at the core
Built in the Kingdom for the Kingdom. We carry that geography in language, in pace, and in the projects we light.
Precise
Engineered, not decorated
Every spec, every tolerance, every IK/IP rating is on purpose. The brand should feel as exact as the product.
Warm

01 - Foundations
Voice & Tone
How we sound
Plain-spoken, technically honest.
NLC writes the way a senior engineer talks to a project manager: confident, specific, and never showy. We do not market lighting - we describe it accurately enough that the right buyer recognizes their job.
+
Specific
Use real numbers - wattage, lumen output, CRI, IP rating. Don't say "premium" when you can say "IP65, 80+ CRI."
+
Calm
Statements, not exclamations. We do not need urgency to sell engineered lighting. Lower the voice, keep the eye.
+
Bilingual
Arabic and English are equal. Neither translates from the other - both originate. Project names stay in Arabic.
+
Useful
Every paragraph should help a specifier do their job. If a sentence does not specify, demonstrate, or qualify, cut it.
✓ Yes

Hero LED Floodlight - 200W, 5700K, IP66. Die-cast aluminium body, asymmetrical lens, suitable for facade and stadium applications up to 18 m mounting height.

× No

The amazing Hero floodlight - a revolutionary new lighting solution that brings unmatched performance and stunning brilliance to any space, day or night.

Words we use
specifier, luminaire, fixture, fitting, panel, switchgear, project, certified, assembled, manufactured, delivered, mounted, installed, tested, IP rating, IK rating, CRI, lumens, CCT, K, lm/W, MV, LV, MCC.
Words we avoid
revolutionary, amazing, world-class, cutting-edge, next-generation, game-changing, unleash, transform your space, stunning brilliance, unparalleled.

03 - Color
Usage & Accessibility
The 60 / 30 / 10 rule.
Color discipline is what keeps the site from feeling cheap. On any one screen, color should distribute roughly: 60% neutral (white, warm bg), 30% navy (structure, headlines, dark sections), 10% orange (the eye magnet - buttons, accent words, badges).
60% · Neutral surface
30% · Navy
10% · Orange
When to use orange
  • One primary CTA per section ("Browse Catalog", "Request Quote")
  • The accent word in a headline (one word, occasionally two)
  • Active state of a navigation link
  • Section eyebrow labels (small uppercase text)
  • Iconography accents inside icon containers
  • Stats and data-point highlights
When NOT to use orange
  • Body paragraphs - always navy/dark on light, white on dark
  • Large background fills - orange is a spotlight, not a wall
  • Multiple competing CTAs on the same screen
  • Decorative borders on every element - orange must mean something

08 - Motion & bilingual
Motion & Interaction
Motion is a quiet handshake.
Animations should reward interaction - never distract from content. Everything uses one easing curve: cubic-bezier(.4, 0, .2, 1). Everything is short - .3s by default.
Tokens
UseDurationEasing
Hover (button, card).3 scubic-bezier(.4, 0, .2, 1)
Mobile menu.4 scubic-bezier(.4, 0, .2, 1)
Image scale.4 – .6 sease
Hero slide fade.55 sease-in-out
Marquee (clients)60 slinear, infinite
Hero orb drift12–15 sease-in-out alternate
Patterns
  • Card lift - hover translates Y by –6 to –8 px; shadow grows from md → lg; border fades to transparent.
  • Button hover - Y –2 px; orange shadow opacity .35 → .45.
  • Arrow advance - icon translates X by +4 px on hover (Bizmo arrows / chevrons).
  • Image bloom - scale 1.05–1.08 on container hover.
  • Section reveal - AOS library, fade-up 30 px, stagger 80 ms between sibling cards.
Reduced motion
Respect prefers-reduced-motion: reduce. Disable all infinite animations (orbs, marquees), instant transitions, and AOS reveals.

09 - Practice
Print Collateral
From screen to paper.
Same brand, different substrate. When NLC moves from web to print - datasheets, business cards, tender documents, signage - the rules below keep the identity tight across both.
Datasheets (PDF)
  • A4 portrait, 18 mm safe area
  • Hero product image: transparent PNG, centered, 60% page width
  • Specs table: 8.5 pt Bizmo Regular, 1 px navy rules
  • Header: NLC mark + product name + part number
  • Footer: contact strip, page X of Y
Business cards
  • 85 × 55 mm, double-sided
  • Front: mark only (orange) on navy
  • Back: lockup top-left, name + role + phone + email + URL
  • Stock: 350 gsm matt, soft-touch laminate
Tender / RFP doc
  • A4 portrait, 25 mm gutter on bound edge
  • Cover: navy, full-bleed, mark + project name
  • Section dividers: orange band, 12 mm tall

Do's & Don'ts
The short list to keep on your desk.
DO
  • Use the section pattern (eyebrow → title → sub-deck) on every new content section.
  • Keep one orange word per headline. Pick the most important.
  • Alternate --bg and --white bands for vertical rhythm.
  • Limit to one primary CTA per band, one orange CTA band per page.
  • Use the actual color tokens (CSS variables), never raw hex.
  • Keep product photos as transparent PNGs, centered, with 14 px breathing room.
  • Use the standard radius scale: 12 / 20 / 28 / 50 px (pill).
  • Set body line-height 1.6–1.7 (EN), 1.8–1.85 (AR).
  • Reach for the existing card components before designing new ones.
  • Test contrast against WCAG AA before shipping.
DON'T
  • Don't introduce new brand colors. Tints, yes - new hues, no.
  • Don't use orange for body text on white (fails contrast).
  • Don't stack two orange CTA bands. Once is impact, twice is noise.
  • Don't justify body copy. It breaks the engineered feel.
  • Don't reset the wordmark in another typeface. Use the SVG.
  • Don't use stock photography of generic offices or skylines.
  • Don't use icons without a colored container (raw FA icons feel adrift).
  • Don't mix shadow colors - shadows are always navy-tinted.
  • Don't introduce custom radii. Pick from the scale.
  • Don't write hyperbolic copy ("revolutionary", "stunning", "world-class").
