**Verdict: reachable on organic alone, if the content engine actually runs.** A B2B manufacturer page growing at 12% a month lands at ~10,100 by 31 December from a 6,000 base. At 10% it lands at 9,300; at 15%, 11,400. The target sits right inside the achievable band.

That single fact restructures the plan:

> **Paid is no longer the engine. It is insurance.** The five free engines carry the number; a small paid line exists to buy back weeks lost to slippage, not to buy the outcome.

A 10,000-follower page at 1.5% engagement reaches fewer real buyers than a 6,000-follower page at 4%.

## 2. Week 1: audit the 6,000 you already have

**Do this before anything else is scheduled.** Every number below assumes the existing base is broadly the right audience. If it isn't, the plan branches. Pull from LinkedIn Page Analytics:

| Pull | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Follower demographics — location, job function, seniority, industry, company size | Sets the realistic quality floor at 10K (see §3) |
| 12-month follower growth trend | The passive baseline rate. If the page currently adds 30/month, 206/week is a 30× acceleration — a different conversation than accelerating from 100/week. |
| Engagement rate on the last 20 posts | The single most important diagnostic. Determines whether adding followers helps or hurts. |
| Follower vs. visitor split | High visitors and low followers means the page converts badly — a fixable, cheap win. |
| Top 10 posts of the last 12 months | Free evidence of what this specific audience responds to. Beats any assumption in this document. |

### The three branches

**A · The base is well-qualified** — ≥60% in target job functions, ≥70% KSA/GCC, engagement ≥2.5%.
Run the plan exactly as written. The 6,000 are an amplifier: every post starts with real reach, so the content engine compounds faster than the model assumes and the 11,000 planned outcome is conservative.

**B · The base is diluted** — heavy job-seeker or student mix, scattered geography, or a large share outside the target functions.
The 6,000 cannot be un-followed, so the quality floor at 10K has to be recalculated from what is actually there rather than aspiration. Tighten *every* incoming channel — paid, invites, events — to ICP-only, accept a lower blended floor, and report the **incremental** quality of the new 4,000 separately from the blended number. That incremental figure is the honest measure of whether this campaign worked.

**C · The base came from a one-off spike** — an acquisition announcement, an event, a single viral post — with engagement now under 1.5%.
Treat engagement recovery as **gate zero**. Do not add paid followers until organic engagement clears 2.5%. Adding followers to a page with a reach problem makes the reach problem worse: the denominator grows, the percentage falls further, and LinkedIn distributes the next post to fewer people still. Six to eight weeks of organic-only content, then resume.

## 5. Engine 1 — The organic content engine (now the primary engine)

### Target: +1,300

### 5.1 Why this now carries the plan

At 6,000 followers, each post starts with meaningful organic distribution. A strong post reaching 25% of followers puts 1,500 people in front of it before a single reshare — and reshares from that base are what recruit new followers. This is the compounding loop, and it only exists because the base is already substantial. At 1,146 it barely turned over; at 6,000 it does real work.

That makes **consistency the highest-leverage variable in the whole plan.** Five posts a week, every week, for nineteen weeks, is worth more than any budget line in this document.

### 5.2 The Academy is the content plan

**111 episodes, 12 seasons, bilingual, ~2 minutes each, engineering-verified against EN 12464-1 and SASO, each already scoped with a LinkedIn carousel.** Packaging is the only work left.

The rule: **one episode → one carousel + one short video + one text post + one poll = 4 posts.** At 5 posts/week with 2 from the Academy, nineteen weeks consumes ~38 episodes. There is no risk of running out.

Lead with the episodes that map to real compliance pain:

| Episode | Why it will travel |
|---|---|
| EP30 — Emergency Lighting & KSA Civil Defense | Every consultant has failed an inspection on this |
| EP49 — SASO Certification & SABER | The import gate; procurement teams live here |
| EP47 — Saudi Building Code (SBC) Navigation | Thirteen volumes, universally confusing |
| EP40 — Saudi MV Tariff & SEC Interface | kVA penalties are money |
| EP33 — Type-Tested Assembly (IEC 61439) | A genuinely contrarian, arguable position |
| EP27 — Access Control: Wiegand vs OSDP | Now mandatory on new KSA government tenders |
| EP37 — Cable Sizing in Saudi Heat | Hyper-local; no global brand can publish this |
| EP34 — Arc Flash & PPE | Safety content consistently over-performs |
| EP48 — Mostadam vs LEED v4.1 | Vision 2030 tie-in |
| EP50 — TCO vs Capex | Arms the specifier to defend a premium spec internally |

### 5.3 Content pillars

| Pillar | Share | Source | Job it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Academy / Teach** | 40% | 111 episodes | Follows, saves, shares. The growth driver. |
| **Proof / Projects** | 20% | 3,200+ projects; Haramain, King Fahad Causeway, NEOM | Credibility behind the teaching |
| **Made in Saudi / Factory** | 15% | Factory #2, local content, SMT line | Differentiation vs importers |
| **Compliance & Standards** | 10% | ISO 9001/14001/45001, UL E509202, IECEx, QAR24.0012 | Converts followers into shortlisted vendors |
| **People & Culture** | 10% | Engineers, R&D, careers | Highest organic reach of any pillar; fuels advocacy |
| **News** | 5% | TECHDRIVE, ABB visit, BMC acquisition, Factory #2 | Announcement rhythm |

### 5.4 Format mix

| Format | Cadence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Native document / carousel (PDF)** | 2×/week | Highest dwell time on LinkedIn. 8–12 slides, 1200×1500, navy #24285e + orange #F6851F. |
| **Native vertical video, 45–90 s** | 1–2×/week | Upload natively — **never** post a YouTube link in the body; LinkedIn suppresses external video links. Burn in captions. Pipeline documented in `PROMPTS-ALL.md`. |
| **Text + single image** | 1×/week | Fastest to produce. Best for opinion and standards takes. |
| **Poll** | 1× per 2 weeks | Cheap reach, and it generates the follow-up post. |
| **Newsletter edition** | 1×/week | See §8. |

### 5.5 Posting cadence — Saudi work week

**5 posts/week, Sunday–Thursday.** Nothing publishes Friday or Saturday.

| Day | Slot (AST) | Pillar |
|---|---|---|
| **Sunday** | 08:30 | Academy carousel (week's flagship) |
| **Monday** | 09:00 | Project proof / case study |
| **Tuesday** | 08:30 | Academy video (native, vertical) |
| **Wednesday** | 09:30 | People / factory / behind-the-scenes |
| **Thursday** | 08:00 | Standards take, poll, or news |

Strongest windows: **07:30–09:30** and **13:00–14:00 AST**. Sunday and Monday carry the heaviest B2B attention; Thursday afternoon is dead.

### 5.6 Language policy

- **Caption:** EN first (the opening ~200 characters must stand alone before "…see more"), then a divider, then AR. Consultants and multinationals read EN; contractors, technicians and government read AR.
- **Creative:** separate EN and AR carousels. Never both languages on one slide.
- **Split:** 3 EN-lead + 2 AR-lead per week. Publish the AR flagship on **Tuesday**, three days after the EN version, to double its reach without cannibalising it.
- Arabic creative uses **Cairo**, RTL, per the site's own type system.
- **Judge AR against AR benchmarks**, not EN ones — otherwise it looks like it is failing and gets cut.

### 5.7 Post construction rules

1. **The first line is 90% of the job.** Lead with a number, a cost, or a specific failure: *"A cable sized at 30 °C will overheat in a 50 °C Saudi MEP riser. Here's the derating factor most BOQs miss."*
2. **No links in the post body.** First comment, or edit them in after 30 minutes.
3. **3–5 hashtags**, at the end. Standing set: `#SaudiArabia #Vision2030 #MEP #LightingDesign #MadeInSaudi` plus one topical.
4. **Tag deliberately.** Partner companies, clients only where the post genuinely features them and permission exists, and the employees who appear. Never tag people not in the content.
5. **Reply to every comment within 2 hours** during the first 4 hours. The strongest free reach signal available — and at a 6,000 base it now generates real volume, so budget the time.
6. **No generic stock photography.** 3,200+ real projects and two factories exist. Use them.
7. **One idea per post.**

## 6. Engine 2 — Employee advocacy

### Target: +1,200

NLC lists **1,001–5,000 employees**. Assume ~400 with active profiles and ~250 realistically activatable. The average Saudi engineer carries 400–900 connections concentrated in exactly the target industries. Still the largest free lever the company owns, and still switched off.

### Mechanics

**1 · Claim the employees.** Any profile whose employer field is free text or misspelled does not link to the page. Audit with HR and circulate the exact page URL — this alone unlocks the *Notify employees* button and the employee-reach multiplier.

**2 · Use "Notify employees" once a day**, on the strongest post only, never all five. A 15% click-through from 400 staff is 60 guaranteed engagements inside the first hour — precisely the early signal LinkedIn reads as quality.

**3 · Three tiers.**

| Tier | Who | Ask | How compliance is won |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Leadership (~15)** | GM, division heads, sales director, R&D head, factory manager | 1 personal post/week + 2 reshares/week | Direct sponsor mandate; drafts ghostwritten for them |
| **2 — Commercial + Engineering (~80)** | Sales, estimation, projects, R&D, QA | 2 reshares/week with one line of personal comment | Sunday broadcast with links and suggested captions |
| **3 — All staff (~250+)** | Everyone with a profile | React or comment on 2 posts/week | Monthly internal leaderboard and recognition |

**4 · Make it frictionless.** One WhatsApp/Teams broadcast every Sunday morning: the week's 5 links, a copy-paste caption for each in EN and AR, and one instruction — *"Reshare with your own first line. Do not copy the same comment."* Identical comments get suppressed; varied ones do not.

**5 · Thought Leader Ads.** Sponsor the best-performing leadership post each month. Personal posts convert consistently better than page-branded ads in B2B.

### Guardrails
- Never make advocacy mandatory in writing — it reads as astroturf and staff resent it. Make it visible, rewarded and easy.
- No engagement pods, no "comment 🔥 below" schemes. The audience is engineers and they notice.

## 7. Engine 3 — Page invite credits

### Target: +650

LinkedIn gives each page **250 invitation credits per calendar month**, and **a credit is refunded when the invite is accepted**. Note this engine is capped by LinkedIn, not by NLC's follower base — so its contribution is unchanged from the original plan and it is now a proportionally larger share of a smaller gap.

1. Appoint **10–15 page admins** across sales, projects, engineering and leadership. Credits are a shared page pool but each admin invites their own connections, so more admins means a far better-qualified eligible pool drawn from the same 250.
2. **Spend the full 250 in the first week of every month.** Credits do not roll over. Leaving them unspent is the most common waste on B2B pages.
3. **Invite selectively**, filtered to the ICP in §3. A 40%+ acceptance rate keeps refunding credits and turns 250 into 350–450 sends a month. A sloppy bulk send at 10% dries up immediately and seeds the page with people who never engage — which drags reach on every future post.
4. Aug (partial) + Sep + Oct + Nov + Dec ≈ **1,600–1,900 invites at 35–40% acceptance ≈ 600–700 followers.**

## 8. Engine 4 — Newsletter + Events

### Target: +550

### 8.1 LinkedIn Newsletter — "NLC Academy Weekly"

The most underused follower mechanism on LinkedIn: **subscribing to a page newsletter follows the page**, every edition sends both a notification and an email to all subscribers, and launching one prompts LinkedIn to invite the page's existing followers to subscribe.

**At a 6,000 base that launch invitation is worth substantially more than it would have been at 1,146** — it goes to 6,000 people at once. Treat it as a one-time reach event and do not spend it before the content system is running.

- **Launch:** Week 2 (w/c 24 Aug)
- **Cadence:** weekly, every Sunday. One Academy episode expanded to 600–900 words, EN with an AR summary block.
- **Expected:** subscribers settle at 25–35% of followers — so 1,500–2,000 by year end, each one receiving an email every week.

### 8.2 LinkedIn Events / Live sessions

Four sessions, one a month, 40 minutes, Arabic and English:

| Month | Session | Hook |
|---|---|---|
| Sep | Emergency Lighting & KSA Civil Defense compliance | "What inspectors actually check" |
| Oct | SASO / SABER — clearing customs first time | Procurement pain |
| Nov | IEC 61439 — what "design-verified" doesn't cover | Contrarian, technical, tender-relevant |
| Dec | Lighting a Vision 2030 giga-project: NEOM & Diriyah | Aspirational, NLC-only credibility |

Every registrant can be invited to follow, event pages surface in registrants' networks, and a registration is a far stronger intent signal than a follow. **Target 250–400 registrants each** — realistic against a 6,000 base, where it would have been a stretch against 1,146.

## 10. Engine 6 — Paid, as insurance

### Target: +900 · Budget: SAR 10,000 (of SAR 25,000 total)

**The role of paid has changed.** At a 1,146 baseline it was the only way to close an 8,854 gap on time. At 6,000 the free engines can carry the number, so paid exists to buy back weeks lost to slippage — a slow start, a production gap, a quarter where advocacy underdelivers.

That changes what to buy. **Do not lead with raw Follower Ads.** Lead with sponsored content that also builds engagement, because the constraint at this base is quality and reach, not headcount.

| Campaign | Objective | SAR | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Sponsored documents & video** | Engagement | 5,000 | Promote the top 2 organic Academy carousels each month. Proven creative, higher follower quality, and it lifts the engagement rate rather than diluting it. **This is now the primary paid line.** |
| **B — Thought Leader Ads** | Engagement | 2,500 | Sponsor the best leadership post monthly. |
| **C — Follower Ads** | Followers | 1,500 | Held in reserve; deploy only against a missed gate. Verify format availability in Campaign Manager (desktop only) — LinkedIn changes dynamic-ad availability by market. |
| **D — Event promotion** | Registrations | 1,000 | Feeds Engine 4. |

### Full budget

| Line | SAR | USD approx. |
|---|---|---|
| Paid campaigns A–D | 10,000 | ~$2,650 |
| Design & video production (carousels, edits, AR versions) | 9,000 | ~$2,400 |
| Contingency (released only at a missed gate) | 6,000 | ~$1,600 |
| **Total** | **25,000** | **~$6,650** |

Note the shape: **production is now the largest line, not media.** That is the correct allocation when the plan runs on organic content — the money should go into making 95 posts genuinely good, not into distributing mediocre ones.

### Tiers

| Tier | Paid spend | Realistic 31 Dec outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Zero paid** | SAR 0 | 9,300 – 10,100 — genuinely possible, but no margin for a bad month |
| **Recommended** | SAR 10,000 | **~11,000**, with buffer |
| **Aggressive** | SAR 25,000 | 12,000 – 13,000 — only worth it if the §2 audit shows the existing base is high quality |

### Absolute prohibitions

- **Never buy followers from a third-party vendor or a "growth" agency selling follower packages.** They are bots and dormant accounts. They inflate the number, permanently destroy the engagement rate, cannot be removed, and are visible to any client who checks. At a 6,000 base with the target genuinely within organic reach, there is no argument for this whatsoever.
- No follow/unfollow automation, scrapers or connection bots. They risk restriction of the page and of employees' personal accounts — the exact asset Engine 2 depends on.
